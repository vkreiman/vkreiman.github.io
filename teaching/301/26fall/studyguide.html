  1. Vector Spaces
Vector Spaces
  1. A binary operation on a set \(V\) is a rule which, for any two elements \(u\) and \(v\) in \(V\), produces a third element in \(V\). (Produced element sometimes denoted by \(u+v\), \(u\oplus v\), \(u\cdot v\), or \(uv\).)
  2. A scalar operation on a set \(V\) is a rule which, for any real number \(k\) and any element \(u\) in \(V\), produces an element of \(V\). (Produced element sometimes denoted by \(k\cdot v\) or \(kv\).)
A vector space consists of the following:
  • \(\bullet\ \) A set \(V\)
  • \(\bullet\ \) A binary opertion on \(V\) (called addition, denoted \(+\))
  • \(\bullet\ \) A scalar operation on \(V\) (called scalar multiplication, denoted \(\cdot\))
such that for all \(\bu, \bv, \bw\) in \(V\) and \(k,m\) in \(\bR\),
  1. \(\bu+\bv=\bv+\bu\)
  2. \((\bu+\bv)+\bw = \bu + (\bv+\bw)\)
  3. There exists an element \(\bz\) in \(V\) such that \(\bz + \bu=\bu\) for every \(\bu\) in \(V\).
  4. For every \(\bu\) in \(V\), there exists an element \(-\bu\) in \(V\) such that \(\bu + (-\bu) = \bz\).
  5. \(k\cdot (\bu + \bv) = k\cdot \bu + k\cdot \bv\)
  6. \((k+m)\cdot \bu = k\cdot \bu + m\cdot \bu\)
  7. \(k\cdot(m\cdot \bu) = (km)\cdot \bu\)
  8. \(1\cdot \bu=\bu\)
In Definition 1.2 of a vector space above, \(\bz\) is called the additive identity or the zero vector of \(V\), and for \(\bu\) in \(V\), \(-\bu\) is called the additive inverse of \(\bu\).
A subspace of a vector space \(V\) is a subset \(W\) of \(V\) which is itself a vector space.
A linear combination of \(\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\) is a vector of the form \(k_1\bv_1+\cdots +k_r\bv_r\), where \(k_1,\ldots,k_r\) are scalars.
The span of \(\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\), denoted \(\sspan\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\}\), is the set of all linear combinations of \(\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\).
Let \(V\) be a vector space and let \(\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\) be vectors in \(V\). Then \(\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\}\) is linearly independent if \(k_1\bv_1+\cdots +k_r\bv_r=\bz\) implies \(k_1=\cdots = k_r=0\).
A basis for a vector space \(V\) is a set of vectors \(\cB=\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_n\}\) such that
  1. \(\sspan\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_n\}=V\), and
  2. \(\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_n\}\) is linearly independent.
Let \(\cB=\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_n\}\) be a basis of a vector space \(V\), and let \(\bu\) be a vector in \(V\). Then \(\bu=c_1\bv_1+\cdots+c_n\bv_n\) for unique scalars \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\), by Theorem 1.6. The scalars \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\) are called the coordinates of \(\bu\) relative to \(\cB\), and the vector \begin{equation} \begin{bmatrix} c_1\\ \vdots\\ c_n \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation} denoted by \([\bu]_\cB\), is called the coordinate vector of \(\bu\) relative to \(\cB\).
The dimension of a nonzero vector space \(V\) is the number of vectors in a basis for \(V\).
Let \(A=[\bv_1 \cdots \bv_n]\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix with column vectors \(\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_n\), and let \(\bx=\begin{bmatrix} x_1\\ \vdots\\ x_n \end{bmatrix} \) be a vector in \(\bR^n\). Then \(A\bx = x_1\bv_1+\cdots + x_n\bv_n\).
Let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix. The nullspace of \(A\), denoted by \(\nul A\), is the set of all solutions to \(A\bx = \bz\).
Let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix. The column space of \(A\), denoted by \(\col A\), is the span of the column vectors of \(A\).
Let \(V\) be a vector space, \(\bu\) a vector in \(V\), and \(k\) a scalar. Then
  1. \(0 \bu=\bz\)
  2. \(k \bz = \bz\)
  3. \((-1) \bu = -\bu\)
Let \(V\) be a vector space and let \(W\) be a subset of \(V\). Then \(W\) is a subspace of \(V\) if:
  1. for every \(\bu\) and \(\bv\) in \(W\), \(\bu+\bv\) is in \(W\) (i.e., \(W\) is closed under vector addition); and
  2. for every \(\bu\) in \(W\) and scalar \(k\), \(k\bu\) is in \(W\) (i.e., \(W\) is closed under scalar multiplication); and
  3. the zero vector of \(V\) lies in \(W\).
Let \(V\) be a vector space and let \(\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\) be vectors in \(V\). Then \(\sspan\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\}\) is a subspace of \(V\).
A set \(\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_r\}\) of two or more vectors is linearly dependent if and only if at least one of the vectors is a linear combination of the others.
Let \(\cB=\{\bv_1,\ldots,\bv_n\}\) be a basis for a vector space \(V\).
  1. If any vector of \(V\) is added to \(\cB\), then \(\cB\) is no longer linearly independent.
  2. If any vector is removed from \(\cB\), then \(\cB\) no longer spans \(V\).
%\label{t.expandinbasis} Let \(\cB=\{\bv_1,\ldots\bv_n\}\) be a basis of a vector space \(V\). Then every \(\bu\) in \(V\) can be written in exactly one way as a linear combination of \(\bv_1,\ldots, \bv_n\), that is, can be expressed as \begin{equation*} \bu=c_1\bv_1+\cdots c_n\bv_n, \end{equation*} for unique scalars \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\).
All bases of a vector space \(V\) have the same number of elements.
In \(\bR^n\), the following have the same solutions:
  1. The vector equation \(x_1\bv_1+\cdots +x_p\bv_p=\bu\).
  2. The linear system of equations with augmented matrix \([\bv_1\cdots \bv_p\mid \bu]\).
  3. The matrix equation \([\bv_1\, \cdots \bv_p]\,\bx=\bu\).
Let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix, let \(\bu,\bv\) be vectors in \(\bR^n\), and let \(c\) be a scalar. Then
  1. \(A(\bu+\bv)=A\bu+A\bv\), and
  2. \(A(c\bu)=c(A\bu)\).
Let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix. Then \(\nul A\) is a subspace of \(\bR^n\).
Let \(A\) be a matrix with \(n\) columns. Then \(\dim (\nul A) + \dim (\col A) = n\).
